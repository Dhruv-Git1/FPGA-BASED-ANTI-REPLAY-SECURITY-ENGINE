# Writing Style

The full standard for academic and research writing in this repository. This covers reports, papers, experiment write-ups, and any prose that presents results.

The four always-on rules are in SKILL.md: short sentences, active voice, plain words, and no em dashes, en dashes, or semicolons as connectors. The full standard follows.

## Form

1. **Write complete, grammatically correct sentences.** Do not use fragments where normal prose is more appropriate.

2. **Keep sentences short.** Prefer about 25 words or fewer. Break long sentences into two or three shorter ones whenever possible.

3. **Use active voice.** Prefer active voice by default. Use passive voice when the person or system performing the action is unknown or unimportant.

4. **Use simple language.** Prefer clear and familiar words. Do not use complex language just to sound academic.

5. **Do not use em dashes, en dashes, or semicolons as connectors.** Use a period, a comma, or a subordinate clause instead. Prefer shorter sentences when necessary.

6. **Prioritise clarity and conciseness.** Remove unnecessary words without removing important information.

## Substance

7. **Define technical terms.** Define every important technical term when it is first introduced. Do not assume the reader already knows specialised terminology.

8. **Make claims precise.** State exactly what was measured, which data were used, and under what conditions the result was obtained.

9. **Prefer precise statements over vague language.** Avoid words such as "significant", "substantial", "effective", or "better" unless they are supported by evidence or clearly defined.

10. **Do not oversell contributions.** Describe contributions accurately and conservatively. Avoid exaggerated claims.

11. **Separate results from interpretation.** Clearly distinguish what the experiments show from the conclusions drawn from those results.

12. **State important assumptions and limitations.** Do not leave important conditions or limitations implicit.

13. **Cite sources where appropriate.** Cite prior work, methods, datasets, established findings, and other external claims whenever possible.

## Applying rule 9

The rule against vague evaluative words is the one most often broken by accident. It is not a ban on the words themselves, it is a requirement that they be earned.

Not acceptable: "The new pipeline is significantly faster."

Acceptable: "The new pipeline reduced median training time from 42 minutes to 18 minutes on the 2026-03 snapshot, measured over five runs on one A100."

The second version states what was measured, on which data, and under what conditions, which is rule 8.
